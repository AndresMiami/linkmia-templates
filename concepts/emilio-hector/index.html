    Photography

    Alongside painting, Emilio Héctor works in fine-art photography. In 2014 the Cuban American Phototheque Foundation invited him to become a member in recognition of his photographic work.

    His photographs have been shown at the Coral Gables Museum, the Plymouth Center for the Arts in Massachusetts and Black Box Gallery in Portland, and in the solo exhibition Borrowed Instants: A Journey Through the Lens at St. Thomas University (2017).

    The artist

    A mathematician who paints

    Born in Sancti Spíritus, Cuba, in 1950. In Miami since 1982.

    Emilio Héctor Rodríguez was born in 1950 in the colonial town of Sancti Spíritus, Cuba, and grew up in Havana, where childhood visits to the city's museums turned into an early talent for drawing and painting. In 1969–70 he studied with the Cuban painter Jesús Antonio Valdovino Díaz, then entered the University of Havana to study pure mathematics, graduating in computer science in 1976.

    He left Cuba in 1981, spent a year in Paris, and settled in Miami in 1982, where he worked as a computer analyst and programmer. In 2005 he began painting in acrylic with Dominica Alcántara at the Latin Quarter Cultural Center. Since 2007 abstraction has been his central pursuit, developed further in the workshops of Baruj Salinas and Yovani Bauta at Miami Dade College.

    In 2010, to mark a century since Kandinsky's first abstract work, he gathered a group of abstract artists that became the 7 Plus One Art Project, which he still leads as founder and artistic director. His work is held in public and private collections in the United States, Latin America, Europe and Asia.
